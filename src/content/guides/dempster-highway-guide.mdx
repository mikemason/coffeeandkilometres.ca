## Getting there

Getting to the Dempster is an adventure in its own right. We came up from Calgary through northern BC and into Yukon, and there's plenty worth stopping for on the way — don't just put your head down and drive.

If you're on that route, Liard River Hot Springs is a genuine must-stop. And you'll be on the Alaska Highway — the Alcan — for a good stretch, which has real history behind it if you're into that sort of thing. Watson Lake's Signpost Forest is worth a look too.

We picked up a copy of the Milepost, which is the definitive mile-by-mile guide to northern highway travel. It took us a bit to figure out how to read it, but once we did it was invaluable.

## Dawson City

Dawson is the obvious staging point. You actually drive *past* the Dempster turnoff to get there — it's worth it.

Dawson is the center of the Klondike Gold Rush and a lot of the original buildings are still standing. It's a genuinely interesting place and worth spending a couple of days rather than just one night.

**Staying**: The downtown campground is essentially a parking lot, but it's fine. You're walking distance to everything and there are showers and laundry on site. There's a Parks Canada campground across the river that's nicer, but even if you time the ferry perfectly you're adding 30+ minutes each way to downtown. We stayed downtown.

**Doing things**: The Sour Toe Cocktail is exactly what it sounds like and you should do it. The can-can dancers at Diamondtooth Gertie's are worth an evening. If you actually like cocktails (real ones, not tourist tat), go to [Bombay Peggy's](https://www.bombaypeggys.com/) — it's a proper cocktail bar and a bit of a hidden gem.

## Starting the drive

There's a sign right at the Dempster junction that's a classic photo op. Stop here, take the picture, and air down your tires before you hit the gravel.

### Big truck etiquette

There are serious trucks on this road — supply vehicles, fuel tankers. The rule is simple: either drive faster than them (unlikely) or pull over and let them past. We pulled over a lot. They waved. They were generally decent — the odd cowboy, but mostly sensible and respectful.

For oncoming big trucks: pull over and *stop*. This reduces the speed at which their gravel is coming at you. We had essentially no damage — maybe one windshield chip — but we've seen what happens to other vehicles that didn't.

<InfoBox type="warning" title="Towing a trailer?">
Consider rock stoppers, and tape the leading edges of your windows — especially frameless ones. A rock to the edge can shatter a frameless window; tape absorbs enough of the impact to make a difference.
</InfoBox>

## Tombstone Territorial Park

About 70 km in, you hit Tombstone Territorial Park. Stop here — it's not optional.

The interpretive center hands out loaner milepost guides to the entire Dempster (you return them at the other end). Grab one. The campground is excellent and will almost certainly be full, in which case you join the parking lot camp-out, which has its own vibe. We had a good time meeting people and woke most of them up at 3am for northern lights. No regrets.

There are good hikes in the area. Worth a day if you have it.
