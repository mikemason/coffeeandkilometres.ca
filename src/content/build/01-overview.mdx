## The Van

2021 Ford Transit, high roof, 148" wheelbase (non-extended), AWD. Colour: Carbonized Grey Metallic — which was a distinctive choice until Amazon deployed their entire fleet in the same shade.

We bought it deep in the pandemic, which means we didn't really *buy* it so much as inherit someone else's custom order. Another buyer had walked away from a piece of paper in the Ford build queue. We couldn't change a thing about it — we just got what we got.

As it turned out, what we got was pretty good.

## What We Got Right (Lucky)

**High roof.** Non-negotiable for us. Standing up is not a luxury.

**148" wheelbase, non-extended.** The extended version ("big booty edition") adds usable cargo length but costs you in maneuverability and parking. The non-extended 148 fits in a standard F-150 parking spot, which matters more than you'd think in practice. It also fits on BC Ferries without the oversize surcharge.

**Not white.** We got what we wanted, until Amazon decided the same. 

**Upfitter wiring package and switches.** Ford's upfitter package runs pre-wired circuits to a switch bank in the dash — clean, fused, and ready for accessories. A genuine timesaver in the build. Highly recommend specifying this if you're ordering new.

**4.11 axle ratio.** More torque to the wheels at the expense of fuel economy at highway speeds. Worth it for mountain driving. We notice it.

## A Word on GVWR

We have the 9,050 lb GVWR package, which is slightly above the base spec. **We'd recommend getting the highest GVWR available, and we'd strongly caution against building a van that's going to push past 9,000 lbs loaded.**

Ours hits that limit fully loaded: two people, a dog, full water tanks, and a complete Dempster-ready recovery kit. It's fine — the van is rated for it — but there's no headroom left for scope creep, and scope always creeps.

The weight concern also comes up with brakes, which we address more on the [exterior and mods page](/van-build/08-exterior).

## What We Didn't Get

**EcoBoost engine.** We have the base 3.5L V6. The EcoBoost would have been more powerful and arguably more fuel efficient at highway speeds. No regrets in practice — the base engine has been completely fine — but we'd probably spec EcoBoost if we were ordering fresh.

**Tow hitch.** If you think you'll ever want one, spec it from the factory — skipping it is easy, undoing that choice later is not. That said, a lot of van builders now use a rear platform (for bikes, a cargo box, a spare tire) that doesn't need a traditional hitch receiver, so skipping it is a legitimate choice. Just make it consciously.

**A decent radio.** We have the most basic Ford infotainment system. It works. It's not good. Van number 2 will have CarPlay from the factory.

**Adaptive cruise, blind spot alert, parking assist.** These exist on higher trim Transits. We didn't get them. We miss them. Van number 2 will have these. (Sensing a theme.)

## Ford vs. Sprinter — The Question Everyone Asks

We're in Canada, which narrows it down fast: if you want 4x4 or AWD in a full-size van, your options are essentially the Ford Transit AWD or the Mercedes Sprinter 4x4. We test drove both.

The Transit drives like a big minivan. The Sprinter drives like a large van. If you've driven both you know what that means; if you haven't, the Ford is just noticeably more car-like in feel.

Beyond the drive, a few things pushed us toward Ford:

- **Servicing.** Ford dealers are everywhere. Sprinter servicing is a more specialized and sometimes more expensive affair.
- **DEF and limp mode.** Sprinter diesel owners sometimes deal with diesel exhaust fluid issues that can trigger limp mode at inconvenient times. Anecdotally this seems less common now, and plenty of people run Sprinters without issue — but it was a factor in our thinking.
- **Cost.** The Ford came in meaningfully cheaper.

Honestly though? We're Ford people. That's also a valid reason.

**One genuine Sprinter advantage worth noting:** the 144" Sprinter 4x4 (and more recently, the AWD models) has significantly better factory approach and departure angles than the Transit. If serious off-pavement capability is your priority, that's a real difference. The Transit can be improved — [we did improve it](/van-build/08-exterior) — but the Sprinter starts from a better place geometrically.

## What About Bigger Vans?

The Transit 148 extended or Sprinter 170 would give you more living space. We think about this occasionally, especially as the appeal of a slightly more comfortable bedroom grows. But for now, the mid-size wheelbase feels like the sweet spot: it fits on ferries without surcharge, parks in normal spots, handles backcountry roads without constant anxiety, and doesn't feel like you're driving a bus. 

We'd reconsider for van number 2. But we haven't yet.
