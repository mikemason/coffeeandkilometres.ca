## Going 3D

The thing people get wrong about van storage is thinking in flat terms — a shelf here, a drawer there. Space in a van is three-dimensional. Stuff goes above things, below things, behind things, inside things that are inside other things. Every cubic inch is doing a job, or it's wasted.

Accessibility matters just as much as volume. Digging through a pile at 7am in a campground is miserable. Having one thing in front of another is fine. Having to unload half the van to find the dog bowl is not.

## Inside the van

The piece of storage we're happiest with is what we call the beer cubby. Under the passenger-side foot of the bed there's a stretch of dead space that most builds just ignore. We boxed it in with plywood, added a latch door, and it goes back further than you'd expect — extends back and sits on top of the water tank. Solid enough for heavier items. We store beer there that hasn't made it into the fridge yet, which is where the name comes from.

The cavity between the bench seat and the water tank holds dog food and dog gear. Another spot that would have been nothing.

The tech cubby came later, after we'd spent too many mornings digging through a fabric box on the floor looking for the Starlink or the drone batteries. Building a proper dedicated space for that stuff was one of those fixes that seems minor and turns out to change daily life.

The kitchen galley has drawers for pots and pans and a slide-out pantry. We don't have overhead storage above the front seats — our Transit didn't come with the factory option that makes that work, and adding it after the fact is more involved than it sounds. The headroom up front is noticeably good as a result, and the van feels more open. We wouldn't change it.

## The garage

We left the rear garage open when we built the van and figured it out as we went. After enough trips, we knew exactly what actually lived back there, and we built a plywood crisscross divider to match.

Right two-thirds: long cubbies sized specifically for our gear. Fold-up table, two-person camp chair, outdoor cooking stuff, water accessories, 10lb propane tank. Everything slots in.

Left third: six Rubbermaid bins, each labeled — fix-it box, dog box, shoebox, and so on. We sized them by pulling everything out of the van in a Canadian Tire parking lot, measuring the space, and going inside to figure out what combination of bins would actually fit. Not a glamorous process. Works perfectly.

## External storage

No external storage box yet. A proper ruggedized platform and box for the back of a Transit is a few thousand dollars when done right, and we haven't gotten there. It's on the list for this van and would definitely be on van 2 — it adds real capacity without eating your departure angle, which matters when you're taking the van places a Transit wasn't designed for.

We do have a roof deck with tie-down points. On the [Dempster Highway trip](/guides/dempster-highway-guide) we strapped a spare fuel jerry can up there to get it out of the garage. It'll take whatever we need it to.
